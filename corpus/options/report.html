Quarterly operations review

This review covers the third quarter for the warehouse, delivery and customer service teams. It sets out what went well, what did not, and what we will change before the end of the year.

1. Summary

Orders rose by 8% on the second quarter, and the share delivered on the promised day reached 96.4%, the best figure since the new routing system went live. Costs per order fell slightly, mainly because fewer parcels needed a second delivery attempt.

Three changes made the difference: earlier cut-off times for same-day picking, a second loading bay at the northern site, and text messages that let customers choose a safe place for their parcel.

Customer service handled 11,240 contacts, 6% fewer than in the second quarter. The average wait on the telephone fell from 4 minutes 10 seconds to 2 minutes 55 seconds.

2. Warehouse

2.1 Picking and packing

Picking accuracy stayed above 99.6% throughout the quarter. The packing benches at the southern site were rebuilt in August, which cut the average time to pack a mixed order from 3.4 to 2.8 minutes.

2.2 Stock

Stock counts matched the system for 98.9% of locations. The remaining differences were mostly in small items stored in mixed bins, which we will move to labelled trays in October.

Returns were processed within two working days on average. Damaged returns made up 7% of the total, down from 9%, after the change to the packaging of glassware.

3.2 Vehicles

Six electric vans joined the fleet in September. Their running cost per mile is about a third of the diesel vans they replace, and drivers report that they are easier to manoeuvre in town centres.

Two vans were off the road for more than a week waiting for parts. We have agreed a faster service with the supplier for the coming quarter.

4. Customer service

Most contacts were about delivery times and changes to delivery addresses. The new self-service page now handles a quarter of address changes without an agent.

Satisfaction with the answer received was 4.5 out of 5. Complaints about late deliveries fell by a fifth.

5. Plans for the fourth quarter

  1. Move small items into labelled trays at both sites by the end of October.
  2. Extend safe-place text messages to Scotland and the North.
  3. Add four more electric vans, subject to the charging points being ready.
  4. Hire twelve seasonal pickers for the busiest six weeks of the year.
  5. Publish delivery performance on the website every month.
